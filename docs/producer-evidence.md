# Producer evidence, human response and authorized forgetting

A retained listening belongs to its attributed producer. Its listener/pass identity,
claim categories, original payload and apparatus/access declarations are evidence of
that account. Navigators must not silently rewrite a machine payload into a human
account, promote interpretation to measurement, or turn a retained report into fresh
hearing. Unknown access stays unknown; a human response does not calibrate the
original apparatus or establish that another participant heard its source.

Use the existing human-account and revision APIs to add a response or correction.
A human response names the earlier record and records its own access and attribution;
a revision creates a fresh record and retains the previous account. The relationship
view labels these record responses separately from second reports and influence.
Library tags, places and notes are curation metadata, not permission to alter a
producer's listening core. Research acceptance, contestation or rejection must remain
an attributable review with reasons and evidence ancestry; it is not a rewrite of the
source account or an independent confirmation of every inherited claim.

Authorized forgetting removes governed content under the existing deletion and
publication controls. It is not a license to retain a rewritten substitute, reconstruct
forgotten content from another projection, or expose owner-only evidence publicly.
Where a permitted record retains a reference to a now-missing source, show the gap;
do not fabricate the missing participant or decision. Revoking a publication grant
also does not authorize mutation of the retained owner account.

This is the K8 evidence boundary. Existing additive human records, revisions,
source-preserving relationship views and disclosure/revocation tests support it.
The broader K9 research review workflow remains a separate implementation task.
